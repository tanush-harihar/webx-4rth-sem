White Nights

by Fyodor Dostoevsky

A lonely man walks the streets of St. Petersburg at night.
He is a dreamer, not a doer.
He lives more in his head than in the world.

One night he meets Nastenka.
A girl suspended between hope and waiting.

For four nights they share loneliness, dreams, and emotional warmth.
He falls in love instantly, desperately.
Because lonely minds attach fast.

The man she waits for returns.
Reality interrupts fantasy.
Memory defeats possibility.

She leaves.
The dreamer remains.
Alone, but awakened.

White Nights is not romance.
It is emotional starvation meeting temporary comfort.
A reminder that some people fall in love not with others,
but with the feeling of being understood.
